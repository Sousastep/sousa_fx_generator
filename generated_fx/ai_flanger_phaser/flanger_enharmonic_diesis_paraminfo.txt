================================================================================
flanger_enharmonic_diesis — Parameter Information
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
A comb flanger whose notch lattice is placed a *just* interval away from the
played note's harmonics, instead of an arbitrary detune. The taps that make the
comb are spaced by a fixed 5-limit ratio, so the nulls land at
harmonic·f0 / r — the same rungs of the tuning lattice reached by a different
path. Measured with f0 = 55 Hz and harmonic = 3: nulls at 165.0 Hz (1/1),
163.0 Hz (81/80) and 158.4 Hz (25/24), i.e. exactly the comma / diesis below the
3rd harmonic, not "about 20 cents".

On a sustained tuba note every partial ends up just off its null — partly
notched and phase-rotated — which is the enharmonic equivalent of a reese's
mistuned unison: one pitch, two spellings, beating. Glide it and the whole
lattice slides against the note.

It is a tap-sum (boxcar) comb, not a notch on top of the dry signal: the output
is the average of the taps, which passes at unity only at DC and at
taps·harmonic·f0/r, with lobes in between. Partials between nulls are cut too
(at the defaults on a 55 Hz note: fundamental ≈ −2 dB, 2nd harmonic ≈ −8 dB),
so expect a darker, hollower tone, not just thin notches.

Source: Tong & Tojo, SMC-19 (5-limit ratios); Christou, NIME-25 §3.2 (tuning
lattice); flanger structure Esqueda/Välimäki/Parker, DAFx-15 §3 Fig. 6;
fractional delay after Nam & Välimäki, DAFx-09 §2.1.

100% wet, no dry/wet control, no limiter.

PARAMETERS (8)

1. interval — 1/1, 81/80, 128/125, 25/24, 135/128; default 25/24
   The enharmonic pair that spaces the comb.
   1/1 = a plain harmonic comb. 81/80 = syntonic comma, 21.5 cents — the subtlest
   mistuning, a slow shimmer. 128/125 = lesser diesis, 41.1 cents.
   25/24 = chromatic semitone ("enharmonic diesis"), 70.7 cents — the obvious
   one. 135/128 = major chroma, 92.2 cents, close to a semitone of smearing.

2. harmonic — 1…8, default 3
   Comb period in harmonics of the note: 3 puts a null on every third harmonic,
   1 on every harmonic (very dense), 8 on every eighth (broad, granular).

3. taps — 3…8, default 6
   Number of comb rungs. The nulls are full-depth at any count; more taps put
   more nulls (and narrower lobes) between the passbands at DC and at
   taps·harmonic·f0/r, so more = more defined comb and a darker overall tone,
   fewer = broader, brighter colour.

4. detune_sweep — 0…1, default 0.15
   Depth of the glide, in octaves of the lattice (±1 octave at 1). The whole
   lattice moves as one, so the just interval between its rungs — and therefore
   the tuning — is preserved at every point of the sweep.

5. sweep_rate_hz — 0.02…1 Hz, default 0.05 Hz
   Glide speed. Very slow is right: this is meant to be heard as drift, not as
   an LFO.

6. feedback — -0.8…+0.8, default +0.25
   Recirculation of the (damped) comb output. Positive makes the passbands ring
   and also lifts the low end — the comb passes DC, so +0.8 is up to ~+14 dB on
   the lowest partials; trim with out_gain_db. Negative pulls the low end down
   and rings the odd lobes instead. This is the control that turns the effect
   from a colour into a resonance.

7. tone_hz — 600…14000 Hz, default 5000 Hz
   Damping inside the feedback loop. Low keeps it dark and dubby.

8. out_gain_db — -24…+12 dB, default 0 dB
   Final trim.

INLETS / OUTLETS
- in1 / in2: stereo audio.
- in3: f0 in Hz. 0 or out of range = unknown: the last valid value is held.
  Cold start is 55 Hz (A1), so the comb is tuned with nothing patched.
- in4: 0..1 one-bar phasor (not Hz). While it moves the glide runs exactly one
  full cycle per bar and sweep_rate_hz is ignored; held constant or unconnected
  the effect free-runs at sweep_rate_hz. A stepped (non-phasor) signal here
  will jump the glide phase.
- out1 / out2: stereo, wet only.

NOTES
- No detune is ever expressed in cents: every rung spacing is a ratio of small
  integers (81/80, 128/125, 25/24, 135/128).
- Taps are 1/nTaps-normalised, so the passbands sit at unity (see WHAT IT IS
  for what happens between them).
- The tracked f0 is slewed over ~20 ms, so a note change bends every tap at
  once — a short chirp/doppler on the attack is expected.
- At very high sample rates with low notes, harmonic 1 and full glide, the
  comb spacing is capped so the longest tap fits the 16384-sample buffer; the
  lattice then stops following the note downward.
