PaulWide — Airwindows (Chris Johnson), MIT
Page: https://www.airwindows.com/paulwide/

What it is: a dither / bit-depth reducer. It quantizes to the chosen bit
depth using "Paul" dither (the difference of successive random values, i.e.
highpassed TPDF noise, so the hiss sits up high and sounds quieter), with
left and right dither forced to differ strongly, so the noise is spread wide
in the stereo field instead of sitting in the middle. At 24/16 bits it is
a mastering dither; at low bit depths it is a wide, airy bitcrusher.

Parameter:

- bits  (Quant + DeRez)  0 .. 24 bits, default 24
  Quantization depth (continuous: 12.5 bits is a valid setting).
  24 / 16 = the original Quant modes with DeRez at 0. Lower = crunchier
  and noisier. Below 4 bits the output also gets quieter (the original
  limits its output scaling there) and turns into gated, crackly noise.
  The original's two knobs did one job between them: Quant chose a 24- or
  16-bit base and DeRez scaled it by (1-DeRez)^6, i.e.
  bits = base + 6 * log2(1 - DeRez); the one slider covers every setting
  of both.

Port notes: matches the airwindows C++ in a differential test (identical
at whole-octave settings; elsewhere differences are 1-LSB steps from the
original's float32 rounding of its DeRez scale). The random generator
(used for the dither itself) runs on native integer operations —
bit-identical sequence, about 70% fewer ns/sample on a translated test
harness.
