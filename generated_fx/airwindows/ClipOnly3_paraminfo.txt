ClipOnly3 — Airwindows (Chris Johnson), MIT
Page: https://www.airwindows.com/cliponly3/

What it is: a "safety clipper" for the end of a chain. Peaks above
-0.83 dBFS (0.9085) are softened by blending toward a slightly randomized
level (noise-edged clipping, so clipped tops don't sound like flat digital
squares), and the output is held under a slew-aware hard ceiling of
-0.54 dBFS (0.94): the faster the waveform moves, the lower that ceiling
gets, which keeps intersample peaks near 0 dBFS. At normal levels it is
inaudible apart from a one-sample delay.

No parameters. It never adds gain, so on a quiet tuba it does nothing until
something upstream pushes the level near full scale. For a drive control,
adjustable thresholds or the "only what was clipped" output, use ADClip9
(its default thresholds closely match ClipOnly3).

Behaviour to know about:
- Output is one sample late.
- Very fast, loud transients can be held down even below the -0.83 dBFS
  threshold, because the hard ceiling drops with slew (0.94 / (1 + 1.4 *
  slew)); with ordinary bass material this never engages.
- Above 88.2 kHz the clip averaging spreads over more samples, as in the
  original.

Port notes: DSP is sample-identical to the original double-precision path
(differential test vs. the airwindows C++, 0 differing samples). The random
generator runs on native integer operations (bit-identical sequence, about
60% fewer ns/sample on a translated test harness).
