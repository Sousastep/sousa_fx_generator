================================================================================
snare_wire_collision — Parameter Information
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
A triggered snare. A six-mode drum head (circular-membrane Bessel ratios)
is struck by a short stick pulse, and where it lands sets the mode balance:
near the centre is round, towards the rim is ringier. The snare wires are
not just a noise burst. They only sound while the head swings far enough to
slap into them, so their buzz comes in bursts at the head's own pitch and
dies with it. The wire texture is velvet noise (sparse random clicks): turn
it down for a crackly, dusty trip-hop rattle, up for a crisp modern one.
Left and right wires are independent, so the snare is wide; the head stays
centred.

Every hit lands on a slightly different spot (mode balance, contact time,
detune, wire sensitivity drift by a random walk). A retrigger adds into
the ringing head (nothing resets) with a shorter, harder contact.

Source: Torin & Hamilton, DAFx-14 §2.4-2.5 eq. 10-16 (mallet and snare
collisions); Marogna, Avanzini & Bank, DAFx-10 P49 §2.2 (membrane modes);
Werner, DAFx-19 paper 53 (velvet noise); Poirot, Bilbao &
Kronland-Martinet, DAFx-23 paper 42 §5.2 (collisions, randomness).

PARAMETERS (7 + output gain)

1. tune_hz — 35…800 Hz, default 190 Hz
   Head fundamental. 140-170 = fat trip-hop snare; 250+ = tight, piccolo;
   under 80 = a slow, gong-like rattle; 500+ = a tiny tin snare. The head
   level is compensated below ~40 Hz (K-weighted loudness within 1.2 dB
   from 35 to 800 Hz).

2. decay_ms — 40…800 ms, default 180 ms
   Head ring (time to -40 dB for the fundamental; overtones die sooner).
   The wires follow it, because they are driven by the head.

3. snares — 0…100 %, default 70 %
   Wire level. 0 = a bare tom-like head. More wires also damp the head a
   little, as real ones do.

4. wire_gap — 0…100 %, default 50 %
   Tight (0) wires only rattle on the big first swings: short and dry.
   Loose (100) wires keep slapping far down the decay: long, splashy buzz.

5. tone_hz — 1500…12000 Hz, default 5000 Hz
   Lowpass on the wires. Low = dark, dubby; high = sizzle.

6. grain — 0…100 %, default 70 %
   Velvet-noise density, about 300 to 15000 clicks per second. Low =
   crackly, lo-fi grit; high = smooth hiss.

7. variance — 0…100 %, default 35 %
   How much the strike spot wanders from hit to hit. 0 = identical hits.

8. out_gain_db — -24…+12 dB, default 0 dB
   Final trim, last in the chain.

INLETS / OUTLETS
- in1: trigger. A single-sample click (any rising edge above 0.1). No
  velocity: the level is fixed, and variance supplies the movement.
- out1 / out2: stereo (head centred, wires wide).

NOTES
- Isolated hits peak near -2 dBFS at defaults; loose wires with
  snares 100 run about 6 dB hotter.
- No safety limiter.
