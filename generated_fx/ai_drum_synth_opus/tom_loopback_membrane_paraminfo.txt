================================================================================
tom_loopback_membrane — Parameter Information
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
A triggered tom. Four drum-head modes (circular-membrane Bessel ratios) are
each a loopback-FM oscillator: at 0 % fm it is a real-sounding modal tom,
and turned up every mode grows its own harmonic series, a "space" tom
halfway to a synth. The pitch glides down because the head loses energy
(tension follows energy, as in a real tom). A roll pushes it back up. A
filtered noise burst gives the stick or felt attack. The upper modes are
panned out, so the drum fills the stereo field without moving its
fundamental off centre.

A click on in2 is a hand laid on the head: it chokes the ring quickly,
from a gentle fingertip fade to a hard palm grab with a dull thump. The
thump only happens while the tom is sounding.

Every hit lands on a slightly different spot (mode balance, detune, pan
tilt, attack colour drift by a random walk). Mode phases run freely, so
no two hits line up the same. A retrigger ramps each mode from where it
is: no reset, no click.

Source: Hsu & Smyth, SMC-19 §3.4, §4.1, §5.3, §5.5, §6.2 (loopback-FM
modal percussion, tom tom); Marogna, Avanzini & Bank, DAFx-10 P49 §2.2,
§3.2 eq. 20, §4 (membrane modes, energy-based tension modulation, hammer);
Avanzini & Rocchesso, DAFx-01 §2.1 (contact time).

PARAMETERS (8 + output gain)

1. tune_hz — 35…800 Hz, default 110 Hz
   Head fundamental. 35-60 = sub boom; 60-90 = floor tom / dub boom;
   150+ = rack tom; 400+ = timbale / syndrum ping. The level is compensated
   below ~40 Hz (K-weighted loudness within 1.6 dB from 35 to 800 Hz), and
   the fm harmonics thin out at high tunings so they never alias.

2. decay_ms — 100…2500 ms, default 600 ms
   Time to -40 dB for the fundamental; upper modes die sooner.

3. glide_st — 0…12 st, default 3 st
   Pitch rise at full energy; the fall follows the decay. 0 = steady;
   12 = 808-style "pew" tom.

4. fm — 0…100 %, default 35 %
   Loopback-FM brightness. It is strongest at the attack and fades into the
   tail. 0 = acoustic modes; 100 = buzzy, synthetic.

5. mallet — 0…100 %, default 40 %
   Felt (0: 4 ms contact, round and dark) to stick (100: 0.4 ms, more
   upper modes and a louder noise attack).

6. spread — 0…100 %, default 40 %
   Stereo spread of the upper modes. 0 = mono.

7. variance — 0…100 %, default 35 %
   How much the strike spot wanders from hit to hit. 0 = same spot every
   time (the free-running phases still differ).

8. hand — 0…100 %, default 40 %
   The muting hand on in2. Fingertips (0) ease the drum out in about
   200 ms with no sound of their own. A firm palm (100) chokes it in about
   12 ms with an audible dull thump. The thump follows how loud the tom
   still is: up to about -11 dB re the stick hit right after a hit, quieter
   as the ring dies, silent on a silent drum. The upper modes die first, as
   under a real hand. The hand stays down until the next stick hit lifts
   it.

9. out_gain_db — -24…+12 dB, default 0 dB
   Final trim, last in the chain.

INLETS / OUTLETS
- in1: trigger. A single-sample click (any rising edge above 0.1). No
  velocity: the level is fixed, and variance supplies the movement.
- in2: hand mute. A single-sample click (rising edge above 0.1) lays the
  hand on the head; see "hand". Muting a silent drum makes no sound.
- out1 / out2: stereo.

NOTES
- Isolated hits peak around -3…-1 dBFS at defaults; fm and mallet at 100
  run about 4 dB hotter.
- No safety limiter.
