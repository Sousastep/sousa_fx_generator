JClones_CA2A — RNBO codebox~ port of the JClones CA2A JSFX
Source: repos/JSFXClones/jsfx/JClones_CA2A.jsfx (MIT, JClones)

What it is: a digital LA-2A optical compressor. The sidechain is a small voice
of its own — a 10 Hz low shelf, a 17 kHz high shelf and a 220 Hz peak (the R37
control) — feeding a true-RMS-plus-peak detector. A logarithmic soft-knee law
produces the target gain reduction, which then goes through a four-stage opto
envelope whose slowest stage has a program-dependent attack. A 43 Hz +30 dB
shelf on the compressed sidechain adds the LA-2A's low-end "breathing", and
asymmetric tube saturation (a 1.3-power curve on the positive half, a gentler
linear curve on the negative half) is mixed in proportionally to how hard it is
compressing.

Parameters (original slider names):

- Peak Reduction — %, 0 .. 100, default 50
  Amount of compression. Internally it sets the knee offset to
  (PR*1.03 - 0.03) * -49 dB, so 0 % is effectively no compression and 100 %
  is about -49 dB of range; it also sets the curve's B constant
  (2^(1-PR) + 1.2) and the gain scale applied to the input of the saturator
  (PR^2 * 14 + 1). It is the widest-ranging control on the box — 20-40 % is
  gentle levelling, 50-70 % is classic vocal/bass compression, above 80 % it is
  heavy and obviously pumping.

- Gain — %, 0 .. 100, default 40
  Makeup gain, but with a deliberately non-linear taper:
  ((24^G - 1)/23)^1.35 * 24. At 0 % it is 1.0 (unity), and it climbs steeply —
  most of the range is in the top third of the slider, exactly like the
  hardware's stepped gain switch. Choose the setting that restores level after
  Peak Reduction.

- Mode — compress / limit, default compress
  Compress = a 1.2 ratio law and a 0.79 exponent on the final gain curve.
  Limit = a 1.3 ratio law and a 0.94 exponent, i.e. a steeper curve with less
  rounded knee, so it grabs harder and rides closer to the threshold. The
  difference is subtle at low Peak Reduction and obvious at high settings.

- R37 — %, 0 .. 100, default 50
  High-frequency sidechain emphasis. Internally inverted (R37 100 % becomes 0),
  and it sets the gain of the 220 Hz peak in the detector path to
  R37_internal * -6.5 dB. So R37 at 100 % means no 220 Hz scoop (the detector
  hears a flatter signal, so sibilance and HF trigger it more) and R37 at 0 %
  means -6.5 dB at 220 Hz (de-emphasised, so the compressor reacts more to
  lows and mids). Changing it also rebuilds the detector filter set.

- Opto Cell — classic / fast, default fast
  Selects the two photocell response personalities. classic = the slower
  original behaviour: the third envelope stage's value is not scaled, the
  fourth stage's ceiling is input*12, and its attack speed is not boosted.
  fast = the value is divided by 5.5, the ceiling drops to input*6 and the
  dynamic attack coefficient is pinned to 0.9, giving a quicker, more grabby
  response. Classic is the more "LA-2A" sound; fast is easier to hear working.

Taper summary: both level controls are percentages with internal exponential
mappings — Gain is the steepest (a 1.35-power curve over a 24x gain range), Peak
Reduction is linear but feeds a 49 dB range. Mode, Opto Cell are switches and R37
is a linear percentage with an inverted effect on the 220 Hz detector peak.

Port notes: all eight biquads and their coefficient formulas are transcribed
exactly (including the 0.187/0.42 peak trick and the paired-stage copies). The
`getFilter1K(0.0)` calls in the source evaluate to the limit 1.0; they are
special-cased rather than dividing by zero. The GR meter was dropped.
