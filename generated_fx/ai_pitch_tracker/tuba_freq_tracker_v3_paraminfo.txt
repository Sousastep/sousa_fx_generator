================================================================================
tuba_freq_tracker_v3 — Parameter Information
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
A low-latency rewrite of tuba_freq_tracker_v2's pitch detection, tuned on six
recorded tuba runs (two-octave F major scales at about 4 notes per second, and
slow chromatic descents from Bb2 down to D1).

How it works: every candidate period (lag) gets its own analysis window,
window_periods times that lag long. A high note is therefore recognised from a
few of its own cycles, instead of waiting for a window sized for pedal tones.
Each lag keeps running sums that are updated at about 11 kHz, and the
normalised square difference function (NSDF, from the YIN / McLeod family)
picks the first strong peak. After that there is very little post-processing:
small pitch moves go straight through, big jumps must hold for confirm_ms,
exact octave jumps must hold for octave_ms, and the pitch is held between
notes.

Sources: von dem Knesebeck & Zoelzer, DAFx-10 (YIN, normalised
autocorrelation, tracker latency); Derrien, DAFx-14 Sec. 2 (the latency floor
is about one to two periods of the lowest note); McLeod & Wyvill, ICMC 2005
(NSDF and key-maximum picking).

Measured offline on the six recordings (149 notes; v2 in brackets). "Time to
correct note" is how long the output takes to stay within +/-50 cents of the
note's steady pitch:
- time to correct note, median: 40 ms (73 ms)
- time to correct note, slowest 10%: 88 ms (136 ms)
- fast F-major runs: time 30 ms (54 ms), in-note accuracy 94% (72%)
- octave errors: 0.0%
Part of the remaining attack time is the player's own scoop into the note.
The C++ prototype and this codebox produce the same out1 through the RNBO
compiler. These numbers come from the recordings only; how it feels live is
for you to judge.

INLETS & OUTLETS
- in1 / in2: stereo (or mono on in1) tuba input. Unlike v2 there is no pitch
  hint inlet: averaging with fluid.pitch~ would bring its latency back.
- out1: tracked f0 in Hz. It reads 58.27 Hz (Bb1) until the first note, then
  holds the last valid pitch through rests. Patch it into the pitch (in3)
  inlet of the effects.
- out2: confidence [0..1], the NSDF peak height smoothed over 5 ms (0 below
  the gate).
- out3: phase-locked phasor [0..1) at f0 (drop-in for phasor~).
- out4: phase-locked sine [-1..1] at f0, scaled by confidence.

PARAMETERS (8)

1. min_freq_hz
   - Range: 24 to 120 Hz (default 27 Hz, just under pedal Bb0 at 29.1 Hz).
   - The lowest pitch searched; the longest lag is set from this. Raising it
     (to 38 Hz, say, if you never go below D1) shortens the lag loop and saves
     CPU. Changing it restarts the analysis for about 0.15 s.

2. max_freq_hz
   - Range: 150 to 700 Hz (default 420 Hz).
   - The highest pitch searched. Notes above it get read an octave low.

3. gate_db
   - Range: -70 to -20 dB (default -48 dB, measured on a 5 ms RMS after a
     22 Hz highpass).
   - Below this level the pitch is held and confidence drops to 0. On the
     recordings, -50 to -40 dB worked and -55 dB or lower started latching
     onto room noise and release noises. Set it just above your rig's noise
     floor.

4. voicing
   - Range: 0.2 to 0.9 (default 0.4).
   - How periodic the sound must be (NSDF peak height) before a pitch is
     accepted. Lower values catch attacks sooner but let in more breathy
     junk; above 0.6 the attacks get slower.

5. window_periods
   - Range: 1 to 5 (default 3).
   - The analysis window for each candidate pitch, in periods of that pitch
     (never shorter than 5 ms). Shorter is not faster in practice: at 1 to
     1.5 periods the noisy start of each note produced more wrong picks, and
     latency got worse. 2.5 to 4 is the sweet spot. Changing it restarts the
     analysis for about one window.

6. confirm_ms
   - Range: 0 to 40 ms (default 10 ms).
   - A jump of 1.5 semitones or more is only accepted once the new pitch has
     held (within 0.35 semitone) for this long. Smaller moves (vibrato,
     bends, scoops) pass immediately. Lower = snappier leaps but more
     glitches from release noises; 6 to 10 ms tested best.

7. octave_ms
   - Range: 0 to 80 ms (default 25 ms).
   - Extra hold for exact octave jumps (within 0.6 semitone of 12). This
     stops the brief drop to the lower octave at tongue stops between fast
     notes. Real octave leaps get up to this much extra delay, so lower it if
     you play a lot of octave riddim patterns and can tolerate an occasional
     wrong-octave blip.

8. smooth_ms
   - Range: 0 to 50 ms (default 1 ms).
   - Glide on out1 in the log-frequency domain (0 = hard steps at the
     ~0.36 ms analysis rate). Every ms added here adds delay to note changes.

PORT NOTES
- The C++ prototype is run with the same settings as the defaults above.
  Through the RNBO-generated C++, out1 matches the prototype to float32
  resolution at 44.1 kHz. At 48 kHz the scores are the same.
- Decimation is automatic: about 11 kHz internal rate at any sample rate
  (factor 4 at 44.1/48 kHz, 9 at 96 kHz).
- CPU: about 25% more than v2 on the offline harness (indicative only; check
  Max's CPU meter).
- The phasor/sine section is v2's Hilbert phase lock, but its isolating
  bandpass is now a linear TPT filter at Q ~ 3.3 instead of two saturating
  svf~ stages at Q ~ 35. It settles in a few periods rather than hundreds of
  milliseconds.
