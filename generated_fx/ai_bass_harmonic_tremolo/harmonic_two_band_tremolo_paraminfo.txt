================================================================================
harmonic_two_band_tremolo — Parameter Information
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
A two-band harmonic tremolo. The note is split into a low band and a high band
at a multiple of the tracked pitch, and the two bands are turned up and down in
antiphase by one LFO: as the low end closes, the top opens. On a tuba that reads
as the note rocking between "body" and "bite" rather than the whole sound
ducking, and the upper partials swing harder than the fundamental — exactly the
behaviour the original Hammond tremolo has, where the effect was deliberately
stronger on high partials (Pekonen & Välimäki, DAFx-11 §1-2).

"depth" is the dry/wet, but there is no dry signal to cross-fade. The split is
complementary (low + high = input exactly), so at depth 0 both band gains are
1.0 and the output is the input sample for sample. Turning depth up splits the
two band gains apart symmetrically; nothing is ever summed with a delayed or
phase-shifted copy, so there is no comb notch and no level dip at the middle of
the knob.

Source: Disch & Zölzer, DAFx-99 §2 Eqs. 1-2 (AM and ring-modulation laws);
Pekonen & Välimäki, DAFx-11 §1-2 (5 Hz tremolo, high-partial bias);
Huovilainen, DAFx-05 §2 (phase-split pumping family).

PARAMETERS (6 + output gain)

1. rate_hz — 0.05…12 Hz, default 3.2 Hz
   Tremolo speed. 0.1-1 Hz is a slow breathe, 3-6 Hz is the classic amp
   tremolo, above 8 Hz it starts to fuse into a buzz.

2. depth — 0…1, default 0.85
   Phase-coherent dry/wet. 0 = untouched input (the two bands still sum to it),
   0.85 = a deep, obviously pumping tremolo, 1.0 = full antiphase swing with
   both band gains reaching 2x and 0x.

3. spread — 0…1, default 0.5
   Phase offset between the left and right LFO, 0 to half a cycle (180 deg).
   0 is a mono tremolo; 0.5 makes the two channels breathe against each other,
   which reads as width on a sustained note.

4. shape — 0…1, default 0.25
   LFO wave shape, sine to square-ish. The knob blends the sine with its cube
   (sin^3), which dwells at the extremes and crosses the middle quickly: at 0
   the tremolo is soft and round, at 1 it is closer to an on/off gate.

5. split_ratio — 2/1, 3/1, 5/2, 4/1, 6/1, 8/1; default 3/1
   Where the bands split, as a 5-limit multiple of the tracked f0. The split is
   smoothed over 30 ms, so it glides with the note instead of jumping. Low
   values (2/1) leave only the fundamental in the low band; high values (8/1)
   keep most of the note's body in one band and tremolo only the top.

6. bar_sync — 0…1, default 0.7
   How far the tremolo is organised by the bar phasor. At 0 the LFO runs at
   rate_hz. Turning it up pulls the rate to the nearest whole number of cycles
   per bar and locks the phase to the downbeat; the settle is a 200 ms glide in
   frequency, never a jump in gain. At 1 the pattern repeats identically every
   bar, and the rate knob picks the multiple.

7. out_gain_db — -24…+12 dB, default 0 dB
   Final trim, the last thing in the chain. Does not count toward the
   8-parameter limit.

INLETS / OUTLETS
- in1 / in2: stereo audio.
- in3: tracked f0 in Hz (20…1200). 0 or out of range holds the last valid
  value; cold start is 55 Hz (A1), so the split is sensible with nothing
  patched.
- in4: 0..1 one-bar phasor. bar_sync pulls the rate to the nearest whole number
  of cycles per bar and phase-locks to the downbeat; held at 0 the LFO runs at
  rate_hz.
- out1 / out2: stereo audio, exactly dry at depth = 0.
- out3: transient click, 0..1, one sample on each detected attack (fast vs slow
  rectified envelope, 3 ms / 60 ms). Audio-rate control signal, not audio.

NOTES
- The high band is the exact complement of the filtered low band, so the two
  always reconstruct the input at depth 0; there is no allpass stage and no
  group-delay mismatch between bands.
- Band gains are floored at 0 so a deep setting cannot invert a band's polarity.
- No safety limiter, no metering, no presets.
