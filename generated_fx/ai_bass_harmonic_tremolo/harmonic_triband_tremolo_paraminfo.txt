================================================================================
harmonic_triband_tremolo — Parameter Information   (rev 2)
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
A three-band harmonic tremolo that rotates. The note is split into low, mid and
high bands at two 5-limit multiples of the tracked pitch, and each band's gain
is breathed at its own LFO phase. At rotation 0 all three bands pump together
(an ordinary tremolo); as rotation approaches 1 the three LFOs sit a third of a
cycle apart, their gains sum to a constant, and the whole-signal level stops
pumping while the colour of the note travels up and down the harmonic series.
That is the spectral-timbre half of what the DAFx-06 effect taxonomy calls
"tremolo" (loudness / rhythm / spectral timbre).

Defaults are set for tuba and other low brass: slow rotation, the fundamental
mostly held, the 2nd-3rd partials ("body") and the 4th-and-up partials ("bite")
trading places.

WHAT CHANGED FROM REV 1
- Bands are now in phase. The old split (differences of cascaded one-poles)
  overlapped heavily and the three bands had different phase, so changing their
  gains also phase-modulated the partials near the edges (a vibrato/phaser
  flavour on top of the tremolo). Rev 2 uses two 4th-order Linkwitz-Riley
  crossovers (24 dB/oct). All three bands share one phase response at every
  frequency and their magnitudes sum to exactly 1, so moving a band gain is
  pure amplitude modulation.
- sub_hold no longer breaks the constant-sum property. The upper bands are
  compensated, so at rotation 1 the gain sum is constant for any sub_hold.
- Dry at depth 0 is no longer sample-exact. The output is the input through a
  flat-magnitude allpass (phase-shifted only). This is inaudible on its own,
  but do not mix the effect with a parallel dry copy: the phase shift would
  comb around the crossover points. Use depth to go dry.
- Tuba-oriented defaults (below). Cold-start split is 3/2 of 55 Hz.

Source: Disch & Zölzer, DAFx-99 §2 Eqs. 1-2 (AM law) and §5 (three 120°-spaced
modulators with constant sum); Verfaille/Guastavino/Traube, DAFx-06 paper 107
(tremolo as loudness + rhythm + spectral timbre); Pekonen & Välimäki, DAFx-11
§1-2 (organ tremolo stronger on high partials); Linkwitz & Riley, JAES 24(1),
1976 (LR4 crossover); Zavalishin, "The Art of VA Filter Design" (TPT
state-variable filter used to build the crossovers).

PARAMETERS (6 + output gain)

1. rate_hz — 0.05…12 Hz, default 1.0 Hz
   Rotation speed. 0.2-1 Hz reads as a drifting filter, and is the sweet spot
   for sustained tuba notes; 2-5 Hz is a musical tremolo; above 8 Hz it fuses
   into a buzz. With bar_sync up, the rate snaps to whole cycles per bar
   (1.0 Hz at 120 BPM in 4/4 gives two rotations per bar).

2. depth — 0…1, default 0.75
   How far each band gain swings around unity. 0 = every band gain is 1 (the
   output is the allpassed input, no modulation); 1 = a band can swing from 0
   to 2x. Only at depth above about 0.94, with sub_hold below about 0.2 and
   rotation between roughly 0.65 and 0.98, can a band gain be pushed slightly
   below zero; it is then floored at 0, which very slightly relaxes the
   constant-sum property. Everywhere else the floor never engages.

3. rotation — 0…1, default 0.85
   Phase spread between the three bands, 0 to a third of a cycle each.
   0 = all bands pump together (plain tremolo, obvious level pumping).
   1 = thirds apart, gains sum to a constant, only the band balance moves.
   In between, the leftover level pumping scales with (1 - rotation). The
   default leaves a faint breath of level motion so the effect stays alive.

4. split_ratio — 1/1, 3/2, 2/1, 5/2, 3/1, 4/1; default 3/2
   First band edge as a 5-limit multiple of f0; the second edge is one octave
   above it. Because the slopes are 24 dB/oct, where the partials land is
   predictable. Share of each partial (amplitude) in the low / mid / high band:

     split   partial 1       partial 2       partial 3       partial 4
     1/1     .50/.47/.03     .06/.47/.47     .01/.16/.82     0/.06/.94
     3/2     .84/.16/0       .24/.63/.12     .06/.47/.47     .02/.24/.74
     2/1     .94/.06/0       .50/.47/.03     .17/.63/.20     .06/.47/.47
     3/1     .99/.01/0       .84/.16/0       .50/.47/.03     .24/.63/.12
     4/1     1.0/0/0         .94/.06/0       .76/.23/0       .50/.47/.03

   3/2 (default) puts the fundamental in the low band, partials 2-3 in the mid
   band and partial 4 upward in the high band. 1/1 splits the fundamental
   itself across the low and mid bands. 4/1 leaves nearly the whole note in the
   low band and tremolos only the top.

5. sub_hold — 0…1, default 0.7
   How much the lowest band is exempt from modulation. At 1 the low band never
   moves and only the two upper bands rock against each other (a clean
   antiphase pair at rotation 1), which keeps the bass note solid. At 0 all
   three bands rotate equally. At rotation 1 the total gain stays constant for
   every sub_hold value; the upper bands compensate for the held low band.

6. bar_sync — 0…1, default 0.7
   How far the rotation is organised by the bar phasor. At 0 the LFO runs at
   rate_hz. Turning it up pulls the rate to the nearest whole number of cycles
   per bar and locks the phase to the downbeat; the settle is a 200 ms glide in
   frequency, never a jump in gain. At 1 the rotation repeats identically every
   bar, and the rate knob picks the multiple.

7. out_gain_db — -24…+12 dB, default 0 dB
   Final trim, the last thing in the chain. Does not count toward the
   8-parameter limit.

INLETS / OUTLETS
- in1 / in2: stereo audio.
- in3: tracked f0 in Hz (20…1200). 0 or out of range holds the last valid
  value; cold start 55 Hz (A1).
- in4: 0..1 one-bar phasor. bar_sync pulls the rate to the nearest whole number
  of cycles per bar and phase-locks to the downbeat; held at 0 the LFO runs at
  rate_hz.
- out1 / out2: stereo audio. At depth = 0 the output equals the input through a
  flat-magnitude allpass (phase-shifted, not sample-exact).
- out3: transient click, 0..1, one sample per detected attack (fast vs slow
  rectified envelope, 3 ms / 60 ms). Audio-rate control signal, not audio.

NOTES
- Crossover structure: the input is split at the first edge into a low part and
  an upper part, the upper part is split at the second edge into mid and high,
  and the low part is passed through the second crossover's allpass so that all
  three bands stay phase-aligned. Each channel uses seven state-variable
  filters, so CPU is noticeably higher than rev 1.
- Because the bands have identical phase, the band magnitudes sum to 1 at every
  frequency for a static split (checked numerically). The edges glide with the
  note (30 ms smoothing) rather than jumping when f0 changes.
- Constant gain sum is not the same as constant loudness: a tuba's energy sits
  mostly in the low and low-mid bands, so level steadiness depends on how much
  of the note is held. In a test with a harmonic bass tone at 55 Hz, the level
  ripple at defaults was about 1.8 dB, down to about 1.1 dB at rotation 1 and
  sub_hold 0.7 (versus about 17 dB for a plain tremolo with the same depth).
  Raise sub_hold to steady the level further.
- Individual bands can reach 2x gain at high depth, and the output has no
  limiter; trim with out_gain_db if needed.
- Band gains are floored at 0 so a deep setting cannot invert polarity.
- No safety limiter, no metering, no presets.
