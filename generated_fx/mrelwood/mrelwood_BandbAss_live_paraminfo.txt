BandbAss Live — kick-keyed low-band ducker (rework of mrelwood BandbAss)
Idea from: BandbAss 0.02 BETA, https://mrelwood5.wixsite.com/plugins/bandbass
This is NOT a faithful port (mrelwood_BandbAss is). It keeps the job and
replaces the DSP for live use.

What it is: when the kick hits, only the band below the crossover is turned
down by a set number of dB, then let back up. Everything above the crossover
passes untouched, so the tuba's attack and growl stay put while the sub makes
room. Zero latency. With no kick, the output equals the input in level at
every frequency (the crossover only rotates phase).

Inputs:
  in1/in2  bass, left/right (the mono tuba on both is fine)
  in3/in4  stereo drum audio, left/right. The two are summed and only what
           is below 110 Hz reaches the detector, so the kick keys the duck
           and hats do not. A loud snare or floor tom with energy down there
           can still trigger it; raise kick_thresh_db if that happens.

Parameters:

- crossover_hz  (Hz) 60..500, default 160
  Only what is below this is ducked. 100-160 = just the sub, hardest to
  notice. 200-300 = the whole low end moves, a more obvious pump.

- kick_thresh_db  (dB) -60..0, default -30
  Drum level (peak, below 110 Hz) where ducking starts. The duck reaches
  full depth 12 dB above this, so soft kicks duck less than hard ones.
  Lower it until every kick you want is caught; raise it if snare, toms
  or kick tails keep the bass down.

- depth_db  (dB) 0..36, default 9
  How far the low band drops at full depth. 3-6 = barely audible room for
  the kick, 9-12 = clear but still natural, 20+ = the sub disappears under
  the kick (riddim pump). 0 = bypass.

- attack_ms  (ms) 0.5..30, default 3
  Time for the duck to reach 90 % of its depth. 1-5 keeps the kick's
  transient clear. Under 1 ms can add a small thump on long low notes;
  over 10 ms lets the bass overlap the front of the kick.

- hold_ms  (ms) 0..200, default 20
  Time the duck stays down after the kick falls away, before release starts.
  Raise it for long 808-style kicks so the bass does not creep back early.

- release_ms  (ms) 20..800, default 140
  Time for the bass to come back 90 % of the way. 60-150 follows fast
  patterns without audible pumping; 300+ is a slow swell.

Changes from mrelwood_BandbAss (original names -> here):
- threshold_kick -> kick_thresh_db. threshold_bass, link_thresholds and depth
  (%) are gone: the original turned the bass down to a bass threshold, so the
  amount depended on how loud you played and quiet notes were not ducked.
  Here depth_db is a fixed amount set by the kick alone.
- attack / hold / release were slider positions with their own curves; here
  they are milliseconds. The original's attack 0 moved the gain in a single
  sample; the fastest here is 0.5 ms.
- stereo and channel4 are gone: both bass channels are always processed
  with one shared gain, and in3/in4 are always the stereo drum pair (the
  original used in3 as the kick and in4 as an optional second sidechain).
- The sidechain lowpass is fixed at 110 Hz instead of following the
  crossover, to keep the snare out of the detector.
