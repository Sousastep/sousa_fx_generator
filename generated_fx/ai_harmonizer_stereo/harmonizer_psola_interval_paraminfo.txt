================================================================================
harmonizer_psola_interval — Parameter Information
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
One harmony voice at a fixed musical interval, added to the tuba. The voice is
built by pitch-synchronous overlap-add (PSOLA, DAFx-11 Paper 52 Sec. 4.1-4.2;
DAFx-03 Paper 20 Sec. 2): the tuba's incoming period is marked every cycle, and
grains copied from those marks are re-spaced at the interval ratio. Because the
grain content is not resampled, the formants of the tuba are preserved — a fifth
up sounds like a smaller tuba, not a chipmunk. The left and right sides of the
voice are two grain streams that drift a few cents in opposite directions, so
the voice never phase-locks with the dry horn and has stereo width even when
the tuba arrives as mono.

INLETS & OUTLETS
- in1 / in2: Stereo Audio Input (Tuba). The left voice stream is made from
  in1, the right from in2. If in2 stays silent for 250 ms, in1 is used for
  both sides.
- in3: Tuba f0 in Hz. 0 or out-of-range (outside 20..1200 Hz) holds the last
  valid value; until the first valid value arrives fallback_hz is used (and
  follows the knob), so the effect is usable before the tracker locks. in3 is
  never divided by and never treated as 1 Hz.
- in4: One-Bar Phasor [0..1] (an exception to the repo's Hz convention). Sets
  the drift phase, one cycle per bar. 100 ms after in4 stops moving
  (unpatched, frozen, or held at any constant), an internal slow oscillator
  (0.13 Hz, ~7.7 s per cycle) drives the drift.
- out1 / out2: Stereo Output (dry crossfaded with the harmony voice)

PARAMETERS (6)

1. interval
   - Range: -12.0 to +12.0 semitones (Default: +7)
   - Taper: Linear in semitones (equal-tempered ratio 2^(st/12)).
   - Function: Pitch of the harmony voice relative to the tuba. Negative values
     are sub-harmonies. Fractional values give microtonal intervals. Below
     ±0.5 semitone the voice fades out, where it would only beat against the
     dry signal instead of forming an interval.

2. mix
   - Range: 0 to 100 % (Default: 40)
   - Taper: Linear dry-to-harmony crossfade.
   - Function: Balance between the tuba and the harmony voice. At 0 the output
     is the untouched input.

3. grain_smooth
   - Range: 5 to 60 % of a period (Default: 5)
   - Taper: Linear, added to the two-period minimum grain length.
   - Function: Extra overlap in the grain window. Low values keep the voice at
     full level with sharper articulation; high values smear the splices for a
     smoother, softer double, but overlapping more grains costs level on upward
     intervals (measured at a fifth: about -1.5 dB going from 5 % to 25 %,
     about -4.7 dB from 5 % to 60 %; octave down is unaffected).

4. width
   - Range: 0 to 100 % (Default: 70)
   - Taper: Linear mid/side crossfade between the two voice streams.
   - Function: Stereo spread of the harmony voice only. 0 puts both streams in
     the centre (a mono voice); 100 puts the up-drifting stream fully left and
     the down-drifting stream fully right. The width comes from the opposite
     drift, so at drift_cents = 0 a mono tuba gives a mono voice whatever this
     is set to. The mid component is untouched, so the effect stays
     mono-compatible.

5. drift_cents
   - Range: 0 to 25 cents (Default: 4)
   - Taper: Linear.
   - Function: Depth of a one-bar-rate pitch drift on the harmony voice, applied
     in opposite directions on the two sides (left +, right -), so the two
     sides are up to twice this far apart. It is both the movement against the
     dry horn and the source of the voice's stereo width. At 0 the voice is
     perfectly static and centred (on mono input). The drift phase comes from
     in4 when the phasor moves, otherwise from the internal slow oscillator.

6. fallback_hz
   - Range: 40 to 300 Hz (Default: 82)
   - Taper: Linear.
   - Function: f0 assumed until in3 first reports a valid pitch; after that the
     last valid pitch is held and this knob is ignored. 82 Hz is the tuba's
     low E.

PORT NOTES
- Grain length is two tuba periods, stretched to at least two synthesis hops for
  downward shifts, so the overlap never opens a gap. Output level follows the
  spectral density of the shift: octave-up keeps only the even harmonics of the
  source, so it is about 6 dB quieter than dry even at the shortest grain — use
  mix to balance. (Measured on a 12-harmonic 82 Hz test tone at grain_smooth 5:
  -12 st 0 dB, -5 st -2.1 dB, +3 st -0.5 dB, +7 st -3.3 dB, +12 st -6.4 dB.)
- Each grain keeps the length it started with, so a note change on in3 never
  cuts a window short (no splice clicks on note changes).
- Delay through the voice is 0 to 1 tuba period (0-12 ms at 82 Hz).
