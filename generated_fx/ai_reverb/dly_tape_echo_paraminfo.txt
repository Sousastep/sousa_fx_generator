================================================================================
dly_tape_echo — Parameter Information
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS
A tape echo: tempo-locked repeats through a machine with wow, flutter,
saturation, play-head loss and a head bump. Where dly_dub_echo is a mixing
echo (filtering and diffusion), this one is the transport: the repeats drift in
pitch, compress when they get loud, and lose their top end.

Source: Jatin Chowdhury, "Real-time Physical Modelling for Analog Tape
Machines", DAFx-19 Paper 3 §4.4 (wow and flutter are a periodic timing
imperfection, implemented as a modulating delay line) and §3 (play-head loss
and magnetisation saturation).

100% wet: only the repeats come out.

PARAMETERS (7 + output gain)

1. echo_div — 1/16, 1/8T, 1/8, 1/8D, 1/4, 1/2, 1 bar; default 1/8
   Repeat time as a subdivision of the bar on in4. 1/8 is the half-beat
   echo (250 ms at 120 BPM; 1/16 is the nearest thing to a slapback); 1/8T
   lands ahead of the eighth grid, 1/8D (dotted eighth) is the classic
   syncopated tape-echo setting, 1/4 and up are long trailing repeats.

2. feedback — 0…0.9, default 0.5
   Number of repeats. Loop gain is feedback × (saturation, head loss — each
   ≤ 1), so even 0.9 always dies away (measured: an impulse falls ~70 dB in
   15 s at 1/8). The saturation and the head-loss filter sit in the loop, so
   loud repeats self-compress and each one is darker than the last.

3. wow_flutter — 0…1, default 0.35
   Depth of the transport drift: 0.7 Hz wow (up to ~2.2 ms) plus 6.3 Hz flutter
   (up to ~0.8 ms). At 0 the machine runs perfectly; 0.3–0.5 is a well-used
   deck; above 0.7 it is seasick on purpose. The right head drifts the opposite
   way for a tape-azimuth effect.

4. saturate — 0…1, default 0.4
   Drive into the loop's soft clipper (unity small-signal gain, so it cannot
   add loop gain). The level it takes off is made up at the output, calibrated
   on a tuba at -18 dBFS RMS: across the whole range the echo level stays
   within ~0.6 dB, so this changes tone and compression rather than level
   (quieter sources come up by up to ~3 dB at 1). High settings make the
   repeats thicken and then flatten.

5. tone_hz — 800…12000 Hz, default 5000 Hz
   One-pole play-head loss, inside the loop. 2–3 kHz is a worn head on a slow
   machine; 8 kHz+ keeps the repeats bright. Slider tapered (exponent 2).

6. head_bump — 0…1, default 0.4
   Amount of the 90 Hz playback-coil resonance (Q ≈ 1.2) added to the output.
   This is the low "thump" that makes tape echo feel bigger than the dry
   signal. It is applied on the way out only, not in the feedback loop (in the
   loop it made the repeats grow without bound), so it does not pile up from
   repeat to repeat. On the tuba it adds ~+1 dB at 0.4 and ~+4 dB at 1; on a
   sub-heavy rig keep it under 0.5.

7. width — 0…1, default 0.6
   Mid/side spread of whatever difference the two heads make. The right head
   runs a 0.15% longer delay and its wow/flutter moves the opposite way, so
   even a mono tuba gets a drifting stereo image; at 0 the echo is mono.

8. out_gain_db — -24…+12 dB, default 0 dB
   Final output trim. Does not count toward the 8-param limit.

INLETS / OUTLETS
- in1 / in2: stereo audio.
- in3: connected but unused (batch inlet layout).
- in4: one-bar phasor. The bar period is measured from how fast in4 moves
  (re-estimated every quarter bar, so it locks within a quarter bar from any
  start phase) and held; with in4 static the echo keeps the last measured
  time, or 2.000 s (120 BPM) if in4 was never seen. Backward jumps and large
  forward steps (transport relocate) are ignored.
- out1 / out2: stereo repeats only.

NOTES
- Delay time is slewed (~50 ms time constant, the head never runs backwards
  or faster than double speed), so a subdivision change glides like a tape
  speed change — up to an octave of bend — instead of clicking.
- Output level: the echo tail is normalised to the input energy
  (√(1 − feedback²)); on the tuba the default sits ~0.7 dB under the dry
  level, feedback 0.9 ~5 dB under (the loop filters eat the long tail).
- Maximum delay is 262100 samples (5.46 s at 48 kHz, 2.73 s at 96 kHz);
  longer subdivisions clamp.
- No bias/hiss modelling, no pitch tracking, no limiter, no dry path.
